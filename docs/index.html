The assessment mechanic

01 Decision Intelligence Framework on the client side. A published, peer-reviewable framework with a 96-source evidence register (decision-intelligence-framework) that gives the client's own data a governed, auditable shape from day one — not a slideware maturity model.

02 Repository-owned twins as the working surface. Every engagement is a durable repository: ingested source material, extracted entities, defect register, and versioned artifacts. What we did on day two is still inspectable on day two hundred. That is what lets synthesis happen in days instead of weeks.

03 Semantic-model discipline as a first-class output. Every assessment names where the customer's business meaning currently lives, what it would cost to move it, and what it protects to keep it inside SAP. That is the input the account rep is missing today.

04 Human-authorized decision artifacts. Nothing goes to the client without a named human signature on the reasoning. The speed comes from the tooling; the accountability stays with a person.

Anchor case — the S/4 margin gap

At Epic Systems we worked the specific problem that a real slice of S/4 customers now sit inside: they did a lift-and-shift to S/4 and never activated the Universal Journal for margin analysis. Their financials moved; their margin intelligence did not. In most SI hands, reconstituting that history becomes a meal — and mid-cap customers rarely have the budget, the internal capacity, or the tolerance for that kind of engagement.

SAP's own guidance is clear: package the fix as a custom configuration in SAP Datasphere. SCS would deliver that guidance as a pre-packaged solution — a shipped configuration, not a bespoke project. Our people can stand it up in Datasphere and close the margin-analysis gap quickly, at a cost the mid-cap customer can actually clear.

Why this protects SAP, not just SCS

The strategic risk in enterprise AI right now is customers lifting the semantic model out of SAP and porting it straight into a hyperscaler. Once that move is made, SAP's long-term position at the account is materially weakened — and it rarely comes back.

SCS is SAP-first by default and fluent in Microsoft, Google, and AWS deployments. That combination lets us represent SAP's semantic capabilities firmly inside the customer's AI conversation — keeping the model where it belongs while the customer still gets the AI answer they are asking for.

The Hyderabad–Bangalore delivery lane

The North American skills that SAP presales most needs — data-model architects, semantic engineers, S/4 and Datasphere-adjacent AI practitioners — are the hardest to source locally and the slowest to scale. Hyderabad and Bangalore together hold probably the highest concentration of data-oriented enterprise resources in the world, and the adoption curve for SAP's new AI tooling is running ahead there of anywhere in North America.

This is not a fresh partnership stood up for a pitch. It is a 15-year working community — a network of practitioners who already know each other one way or another — and the reason we can put hands on hard-to-find SAP and data skills faster than most. That access is asymmetric on the delivery side, and it is the piece a Gold Partner relationship formalises rather than creates.

Gold Partner arbitration, not offshore staffing

SCS would work with an SAP Gold Partner in India as the delivery bench. The role is arbitration: SCS shapes and signs off on the work in North America; the Gold Partner supplies the qualified hands out of the same community we have been delivering with for 15 years; every artifact still lands in the client-facing repository with a named human on it.

That structure gives SAP presales access to skills that would otherwise take months to assemble domestically — under a partner already inside SAP's ecosystem, not a rate-card body shop.

AI closes the offshore review gap

The traditional offshore-delivery risk is context drift: the offshore team quietly substitutes its own reasoning for the client's. Inside EVE, every artifact is versioned in real time and reviewable the moment it is produced. Reasoning does not leave the repository. Review that used to require a weekly cadence now happens in the same working session, which is why the offshore lane is materially easier to manage under AI than it was even two years ago.
