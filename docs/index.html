For Jeff and Jason — one page, no ask.

Steel Cloud Solutions can support SAP North America presales as an arm's-length advisory layer — helping mid-cap and complex-account customers move on the SAP decisions they already have on the table, with Datasphere as the strategic keystone.

We are not proposing to explain presales conversion, and we are not proposing to displace an SI. We are proposing a three-service model that a customer can adopt without touching the account team, delivered from inside the customer's own four walls, sized so Jason and Jeff can sponsor the smallest one first and grow it.

How the first meeting works

Before anything is billed, SCS runs a free discovery meeting with the customer. The customer sends whatever materials they want us to look at ahead of time, we come in with a pre-discovery outline, we ask to record the session, and out the other side the customer gets meeting notes and — if there is a fit — a proposal scoped strictly to Services A, B, or C. Classic pipeline. Any SAP-side conversation happens after the meeting, not during it.

Three service shapes

Service A — Datasphere technical support and product demonstration

SCS goes into the customer's non-productive systems and stands up the Datasphere functionality the customer would be getting on the licence — most often a test system pointed at production, so the customer's SMEs get a working preview against their own real data before they commit. This is hands-on enablement: configuration, connection, first semantic model, first live report.

The value to Jason's team is a live demo the customer already believes, delivered inside their perimeter, without asking the account team or an SI to carry the burden of a synthetic proof.

Service B — Business-case build for the SAP decision

A directed decision-support exercise, scoped to whatever SAP decision the customer is working through. SCS goes in with our structured tools and works backwards from that decision to the numbers that support it — process, financial, and operational context, gathered inside the customer's four walls, structured into an economic justification the customer's own executives can carry.

It is decision support, not audit. We are not pulling entitlement, we are not grading their adoption, we are not measuring anything for SAP. We are helping the customer get the numbers they need, quicker, to make a purchase they are already leaning toward.

Service C — Executive decision-intelligence assessment

For accounts on a major SAP transformation, the full structured piece: a published, highly respected industry advisor signs the recommendations, and SCS runs the working surface underneath. Data gathering, session hosting, recording, structured capture, blueprint drafting, repository custody — all inside the customer's four walls.

The advisor is one of the senior subject matter experts within our consulting group — a partnership of small consulting firms operating under a general partnership agreement with SCS, which is how we can put the right person on the right account quickly. SCS structures the engagement in a form the customer can buy on a specific capacity: a stated number of hours, a stated number of interviews and meetings, a stated feedback format, a stated set of questions to answer. Recorded sessions and transcripts are handed back in a repository. The customer receives one operational blueprint tied to their real transaction and process data, on one document, one bill.

Where Datasphere fits

Datasphere is the piece of the SAP portfolio that keeps the semantic model inside SAP — the ground floor of Business Data Cloud, the bridge for customers retiring BW and Analytics for Office, and the gate to SAC and Databricks without giving up SAP's control of who sees what.

The play SAP is losing on some accounts is replication out of SAP into a hyperscaler's infrastructure with the semantic model rebuilt on that side; several SIs and hybrid-hyperscaler vendors are running the same shape. In that scenario the customer loses the SAP semantic model as the access spine for enterprise data, and the user-level access controls SAP has built into that spine go with it. Strategically this is the most important thing for SAP, and it is a place an arm's-length partner like SCS is well placed to help — we can call the trade honestly for the customer because we are not on either vendor's paper.

In the mid-cap segment this matters most: those customers do not own the advanced SAP products, they lean on Excel habits that will not survive the retirement path, and they feel stuck. Datasphere is often the only feasible bridge — so the real adoption unlock for a large share of Jason's portfolio is getting people to actually use it. Service A puts a working example of that in the customer's own environment.

Why the posture is arm's length

The customer is choosing continuously between AI inside SAP and AI outside SAP, between data inside SAP and data outside SAP. Those decisions need an advisor who is not an SAP suit and not an SI on paper. SCS can hold that seat honestly because our commercial position is elsewhere — we are a channel provider in an adjacent space, our tools are usable for management-consulting work, and we run service delivery every day.

The DMZ boundary makes this structural rather than promised: authorised read-only extracts, no write path to a system of record, work recorded in a customer-owned repository, changes returned only as proposals for named human approval. EVE is the underlying repository and DMZ technology; on SCS engagements it runs behind the SCS delivery, not on the client-facing brand.
