Steel Cloud Solutions · SAP North America

An arm's-length advisory model.

For Jeff and Jason — one page, no ask.

Steel Cloud Solutions can support SAP North America presales as an arm's-length advisory layer — helping mid-cap and complex-account customers get real value out of the SAP investment they already own, with Datasphere as the strategic keystone.

We are not proposing to explain presales conversion, and we are not proposing to displace an SI. We are proposing a two-service model that a customer can adopt without touching the account team, delivered by senior advisors the customer already respects, with SCS doing the legwork behind them.

Two service shapes

Service A — Adoption & Licensing-Value Review

A third-party assessment of how the customer's existing SAP entitlement is being used, and what is holding adoption back internally. The same question SAP would like to ask, but coming from an independent advisor rather than the vendor — so it reads as value discovery, not as an audit.

Outcome: a short, evidence-backed blueprint that names the specific tools the customer already pays for, the deployment or process reasons adoption has stalled, and the changes that would let the customer realise the value that is already on the licence.

Service B — Executive Decision-Intelligence Assessment

For accounts committed to a major SAP transformation, a framework-driven assessment delivered by a senior industry-recognised advisor from our bench. SCS does the preparation — data gathering, session hosting, recording, structured capture, blueprint drafting — and the named senior advisor delivers the recommendations under their own signature.

All work happens inside the customer's four walls with the customer's own private data. No external data sharing, no privacy trade. The output is a single structured decision-intelligence blueprint that ties the customer's real transaction and process data to the transformation reasoning the SI and SAP are already discussing.

Where Datasphere fits

Datasphere is the piece of the SAP portfolio that keeps the semantic model inside SAP — the ground floor of Business Data Cloud, the bridge for customers retiring BW and Analytics for Office, and the gate to SAC and Databricks without giving up SAP's control of who sees what.

In the mid-cap segment this matters most: those customers do not own the advanced SAP products, they lean on Excel habits that will not survive the retirement path, and they feel stuck. Datasphere is often the only feasible bridge — so the real adoption unlock for a large share of Jason's portfolio is getting people to actually use it. That is a lane we can help with without ever standing between SAP and the account.

Why the posture is arm's length

The customer is choosing continuously between AI inside SAP and AI outside SAP, between data inside SAP and data outside SAP. Those decisions need an advisor who is not an SAP suit and not an SI on paper. SCS can hold that seat honestly because our commercial position is elsewhere — we are a channel provider in an adjacent space, our tools are usable for management-consulting work, and we run service delivery every day.

The DMZ boundary makes this structural rather than promised: authorised read-only extracts, no write path to a system of record, work recorded in a customer-owned repository, changes returned only as proposals for named human approval.

  • Which senior advisor from the bench fits which account.
  • Whether the Datasphere-adoption lane is the first customer conversation to run against.
