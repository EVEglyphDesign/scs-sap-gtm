The Datasphere adoption lane.

One challenge, three sizes — where SCS can help.

The lane where an arm's-length advisor materially moves the number for SAP North America presales is Datasphere adoption. Not the presales conversion problem, and not the account team's engagement structure — those are yours. The specific gap SCS can carry is getting customers to actually use Datasphere, and to see it as strategic rather than as a BW replacement.

Three sizes of the same challenge

Micro — getting people to use it at all

BW is on the retirement path. Analytics for Office — SAP's Excel add-in — moves with it. A very large share of SAP finance and operations users still live in Excel every day. Datasphere is the successor SAP is pointing them at, and it is not landing on its own. Customers need a hand getting the first real model up, the first real report in front of a user, the first real Excel-replacement moment. That is a legwork problem, and it is exactly what SCS can front-end.

Mid-cap — the feasibility trap

Mid-cap SAP customers do not own the advanced pieces of the portfolio. They cannot easily reach for the alternatives that a large enterprise could substitute. That leaves them feeling stuck between an ageing BW estate and a Datasphere step they are not staffed to take. In that segment, Datasphere is often the only feasible bridge — and an advisor who can take them across it is the difference between the account moving forward and the account drifting.

Strategic — the keystone of Business Data Cloud

Once the customer's data lands in Datasphere, the semantic model stays inside SAP. That is the door to SAC and to Databricks with SAP still controlling access and meaning. Datasphere is the piece that lets SAP keep ownership of the intelligent-enterprise ground floor while the customer still gets the analytics and AI options they want on top. Every account that adopts it is an account SAP does not lose to a hyperscaler lift-out.

How SCS shows up on this lane

We are proposing two service shapes. Both are delivered inside the customer's four walls, with the customer's private data. No external data movement, no privacy trade, no reseller paper.

Service A — Adoption & Licensing-Value Review

A third-party assessment of what the customer already owns on their SAP entitlement and why the adoption has not landed internally. Delivered by SCS because it is not an SAP-badged audit — the customer can accept a value review from an independent advisor in a way they cannot accept it from the vendor.

The output is a short, evidence-backed blueprint naming the specific tools, the deployment or process reasons adoption stalled, and the changes that would let the customer realise the value already on the licence. Datasphere sits at the centre of that blueprint for most mid-cap accounts.

Service B — Executive Decision-Intelligence Assessment

For accounts already committed to a major transformation, a framework-driven assessment delivered by a senior industry-recognised advisor from our bench — the calibre of a Lillian Richemont-type profile, matched to the industry of the account.

SCS runs the working surface: data gathering, session hosting, recording, structured capture, blueprint drafting, repository custody. The senior advisor signs the recommendations. The customer gets a single decision-intelligence blueprint that ties their real transaction and process data to the transformation reasoning that SAP and the SI are already discussing — and to the Datasphere-and-above architecture that supports it.

The bench, not a person

SCS holds a bench of senior advisors, and we match one to each account by industry, tenure, and how the customer prefers to be spoken to. Lillian is one reference for the profile. The point is not the individual name; it is that the signature on the blueprint belongs to someone the customer already respects, and the legwork behind the signature is ours.
