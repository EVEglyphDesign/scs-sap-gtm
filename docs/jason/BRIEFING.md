## Three sizes of the same challenge

### Micro — getting people to use Datasphere at all

BW is on the retirement path. Analytics for Office — SAP's Excel add-in — moves with
it. A very large share of SAP finance and operations users still live in Excel every
day. Datasphere is the successor SAP is pointing them at, and it is not landing on
its own. Customers need a hand getting the first real model up, the first real report
in front of a user, the first real Excel-replacement moment. That is a legwork
problem, and it is exactly what SCS can front-end.

### Mid-cap — the feasibility trap

Mid-cap SAP customers do not own the advanced pieces of the portfolio. They cannot
easily reach for the alternatives that a large enterprise could substitute. That
leaves them feeling stuck between an ageing BW estate and a Datasphere step they are
not staffed to take. In that segment, Datasphere is often the *only* feasible bridge
— and an advisor who can take them across it is the difference between the account
moving forward and the account drifting.

### Strategic — the keystone of Business Data Cloud

Once the customer's data lands in Datasphere, the semantic model stays inside SAP.
That is the door to SAC and to Databricks with SAP still controlling access and
meaning. Datasphere is the piece that lets SAP keep ownership of the
intelligent-enterprise ground floor while the customer still gets the analytics and
AI options they want on top. Every account that adopts it is an account SAP does not
lose to a hyperscaler lift-out.

## What Steel Cloud Solutions is offering

Two service shapes. Both delivered inside the customer's four walls, with the
customer's private data. No external data movement, no privacy trade, no reseller
paper.

### Service A — Adoption & Licensing-Value Review

A third-party assessment of what the customer already owns on their SAP entitlement,
and why adoption has stalled internally. Delivered by SCS because it is *not* an
SAP-badged audit — the customer can accept a value review from an independent advisor
in a way they cannot accept it from the vendor.

**Output:** a short, evidence-backed blueprint naming the specific tools, the
deployment or process reasons adoption stalled, and the changes that would let the
customer realise the value already on the licence. Datasphere sits at the centre of
that blueprint for most mid-cap accounts.

### Service B — Executive Decision-Intelligence Assessment

For accounts already committed to a major transformation, a framework-driven
assessment delivered by a **senior industry-recognised advisor** from our bench — the
calibre of a Lillian Richemont-type profile, matched to the industry of the account.

SCS runs the working surface: data gathering, session hosting, recording, structured
capture, blueprint drafting, repository custody. The senior advisor signs the
recommendations under their own name. The customer gets a single
decision-intelligence blueprint that ties their real transaction and process data to
the transformation reasoning that SAP and the SI are already discussing — and to the
Datasphere-and-above architecture that supports it.

### The bench, not a person

SCS holds a bench of senior advisors and matches one to each account by industry,
tenure, and how the customer prefers to be spoken to. Lillian is one reference for
the profile. The point is not the individual name; it is that the signature on the
blueprint belongs to someone the customer already respects, and the legwork behind
the signature is ours.

## Why the posture is arm's length

The customer is choosing continuously between AI inside SAP and AI outside SAP,
between data inside SAP and data outside SAP. Those decisions need an advisor who is
not an SAP suit and not an SI on paper. SCS can hold that seat honestly because our
commercial position is elsewhere — we are a channel provider in an adjacent space,
our tools are usable for management-consulting work, and we run service delivery
every day.

The [DMZ boundary](https://eveglyphdesign.github.io/scs-sap-gtm/dmz/) makes this
structural rather than promised: authorised read-only extracts, no write path to a
system of record, work recorded in a customer-owned repository, changes returned only
as proposals for named human approval.
