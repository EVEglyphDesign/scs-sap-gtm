## Three sizes of the same challenge

### Micro — getting people to use Datasphere at all

BW is on the retirement path. Analytics for Office — SAP's Excel add-in — moves
with it. A very large share of SAP finance and operations users still live in Excel
every day. Datasphere is the successor SAP is pointing them at, and it is not
landing on its own. Customers need a hand getting the first real model up, the
first real report in front of a user, the first real Excel-replacement moment.
That is a legwork problem, and it is exactly what SCS can front-end.

### Mid-cap — the feasibility trap

Mid-cap SAP customers do not own the advanced pieces of the portfolio. They cannot
easily reach for the alternatives that a large enterprise could substitute. That
leaves them feeling stuck between an ageing BW estate and a Datasphere step they
are not staffed to take. In that segment, Datasphere is often the *only* feasible
bridge — and an advisor who can take them across it is the difference between the
account moving forward and the account drifting.

### Strategic — the keystone of Business Data Cloud

Once the customer's data lands in Datasphere, the semantic model stays inside SAP.
That is the door to SAC and to Databricks with SAP still controlling access and
meaning. Datasphere is the piece that lets SAP keep ownership of the
intelligent-enterprise ground floor while the customer still gets the analytics
and AI options they want on top. Every account that adopts it is an account SAP
does not lose to a hyperscaler lift-out.

The specific play SAP is losing on some accounts: the hyperscaler side pushes
*replication flows out of SAP* into their own infrastructure and then rebuilds
the semantic model on their side. Several SIs and hybrid-hyperscaler vendors are
running the same play. In that scenario the customer loses the SAP semantic
model as the access spine for enterprise data — the user-level access controls
SAP has built into that spine go with it. That is the strategic battle, and it
is where an arm's-length partner like SCS can call the trade honestly for the
customer, because we are not on either vendor's paper. We have seen this play
run at a live enterprise account and it is exactly the shape Datasphere is
meant to answer.

## How the first meeting works

Before anything is billed, SCS runs a **free discovery meeting** with the
customer. Classic pipeline. The customer sends whatever materials they want us to
look at ahead of time, we come in with a pre-discovery outline against those
materials, we ask to record the session, and we take notes.

Out the other side, the customer gets meeting notes and — if there is a fit — a
proposal scoped strictly to Services A, B, or C below. Nothing outside those
categories. Any SAP-side conversation on the account happens after that meeting,
not during it.

## What Steel Cloud Solutions is offering

Three service shapes. All three run inside the customer's four walls, with the
customer's private data. No external data movement, no privacy trade, no reseller
paper. The services are sized so a first account can start with A, add B when a
decision is on the table, and go to C only when the customer is committed to a
transformation.

### Service A — Datasphere technical support and product demonstration

SCS goes into the customer's *non-productive* systems and stands up the Datasphere
functionality the customer would be getting on the licence. Typically we point a
test system at production so the customer's SMEs get a working preview against
their own real data — configuration, connection, first semantic model, first live
report — ahead of the buying decision.

For your team, this replaces synthetic decks and vendor demos with something the
customer's own SMEs can already touch inside their perimeter, without the account
team or an SI carrying the burden of a synthetic proof.

### Service B — Business-case build for the SAP decision

A directed decision-support engagement scoped to whatever SAP decision the
customer is working through. SCS goes in with our structured tools and works
*backwards from that decision* to the numbers that support it — process,
financial, and operational context, gathered inside the customer's four walls and
structured into the economic justification the customer's own executives can carry
into their approval.

This is decision support, not audit. We are not pulling entitlement, we are not
grading adoption, we are not measuring anything for SAP. We are helping the
customer get the numbers they need, quicker, to make a purchase they are already
leaning toward.

### Service C — Executive decision-intelligence assessment

For accounts on a major SAP transformation, the full structured piece: a
**published, highly respected industry advisor** signs the recommendations, and
SCS runs the working surface underneath.

The advisor is one of the senior subject matter experts within our consulting
group — a partnership of small consulting firms operating under a general
partnership agreement with SCS, which is how we can put the right person on the
right account quickly.

SCS delivers the assessment in a structured form the customer can buy on a
specific capacity. We tell the customer up front: this many hours, this many
interviews, this many meetings, feedback in this format, this set of questions
answered. We hand back every recording and every transcript in a repository,
and we produce one operational blueprint tying the customer's real transaction
and process data to the transformation reasoning the SI and SAP are already
discussing. **One document, one bill** — nothing more straightforward for the
customer to engage with. It is a way of commercialising senior-advisor work
that has not really been done before, and it is what SCS delivers.

## Why the posture is arm's length

The customer is choosing continuously between AI inside SAP and AI outside SAP,
between data inside SAP and data outside SAP. Those decisions need an advisor who
is not an SAP suit and not an SI on paper. SCS can hold that seat honestly because
our commercial position is elsewhere — we are a channel provider in an adjacent
space, our tools are usable for management-consulting work, and we run service
delivery every day.

The [DMZ boundary](https://eveglyphdesign.github.io/scs-sap-gtm/dmz/) makes this
structural rather than promised: authorised read-only extracts, no write path to
a system of record, work recorded in a customer-owned repository, changes
returned only as proposals for named human approval.
